# TDSP reference implementation: conformance status

A statement about one implementation — the one in this repository — at one point in
time, in the manner of RFC 7942. It is not part of the protocol: the protocol is
[SPECIFICATION.md](SPECIFICATION.md) with the documents in [bindings/](bindings/). What
this file says is verified is verified by the tests in this repository; what it says is
not verified is a known gap, not an oversight waiting to be found.

**Status as of the TDSP 2.0 draft, draft-10 (4 November 2026).** The reference engine is the
four packages of [Appendix F](SPECIFICATION.md#appendix-f-reference-implementation) and
implements the draft. The bridges and adapters are this repository's, carried on for 2.0: they serve the
transport profile of §3.5 (TRN-13) and implement of the bindings' 2.0 requirements what the rows
below say; what they do not yet implement is listed under
[gaps of this implementation](#gaps-of-this-implementation). Where a row rests on evidence
gathered with TDSP 1.4 that has not been repeated with the 2.0 engine, it says so.

What follows keeps apart what this implementation was tested to do ([by
requirement](#by-requirement)), what the protocol by design does not promise ([known
limits](#known-limits-of-the-protocol), which hold for every implementation), and what is not
yet specified, built or shown ([not yet specified](#not-yet-specified),
[gaps](#gaps-of-this-implementation), [evidence not yet gathered](#evidence-not-yet-gathered)).

## How it is verified

The tests are grouped into three levels by what they need
([docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md)):

- **L0** — nothing outside the process: the engine against the in-memory transport, the
  adapters against fake bridges, the bridges against fake messengers, the check tooling.
  Runs everywhere, in CI included.
- **L2** — a real messenger server on the same machine: a local Synapse, a local
  Greenmail, `signal-cli`'s own daemon. Runs in CI and locally.
- **L4** — real accounts at production services: two linked Signal accounts, two
  mailboxes at a real email provider. Opt-in, never part of CI.

Every test of the engine but two is L0: the engine against the in-memory transport, which offers
the cursor and the instance (TRN-18, TRN-19) and injects delay, duplication, reordering,
loss, a moved-on window and a restarted bridge. The two others run it at L2 over two of this
repository's bridges each — Matrix with a local Synapse, email with a local Greenmail
(`engine.test.ts` in each bridge) — and show that a joiner is bootstrapped, concurrent edits
converge and a decision of the creator reaches the member over a real messenger. Two kinds of test carry most of the weight:

- **Property tests** with a seeded generator, so that a failure replays from its seed:
  registers over random histories of three devices of the creator whose wall clocks
  disagree — by minutes, by a day either way, a backup put back under its old tag, a register
  a year ahead — with lost and duplicated messages and members that pass whole states on
  (`registers.test.ts`, 1 000 runs per configuration), checked against what each device had
  seen on each target; any order, duplicates and grouping of registers into frames and
  snapshots, checked against an oracle of the register that holds per target
  (`control-state.test.ts`); and the splitting of updates into pieces over permutations and
  duplicates (`split.test.ts`).
- **The list of [§1.7](SPECIFICATION.md#17-what-must-stay)**, item by item, as scenarios
  (`invariants.test.ts`), with the email binding's timing reproduced in the in-memory
  transport — its floor, its suggested pacing, and the rate one provider was measured to
  allow — since email is the strictest binding.

The draft's test vectors — `packages/document-protocol/test-vectors/frames-draft-10.json` and
`invitations-draft-10.json` — pass against the reference decoder and invitation reader,
`control-draft-10.json` against the reference merge of registers, and
`bridges/email-bridge/test-vectors/member-ids-v1.json` against the email bridge;
the behaviour vectors of §3.6 — `clock-draft-10.json` and `digests-draft-10.json` against the
reference clock, canonical serialisation and digests (the delete-set bytes also against Yjs's
own), `updates-draft-10.json` against the reference reader of updates, and `flows-draft-10.json`, 28 flows in five case groups — answers to all, decisions of two devices of the creator, what bootstraps a device, bounds, Unicode and bytes, and healing at the next occasion —, against the engine driven through each on a fake transport and a fake clock (`test-vectors.test.ts`,
`update-vectors.test.ts`, `flow-vectors.test.ts`); they are behaviour, not wire, and not in the wire lock;
`wire/frames-draft-10.json` and `wire/invitations-draft-10.json` freeze what the encoder writes.
Every example frame in §4.5 and Appendix D is what the encoder writes, byte for byte, with
`2` in place of the draft marker the encoder writes (`spec-examples.wire.test.ts`), and
Appendix D's Signal envelope what the Signal bridge sends. The wire lock (`wire/wire-lock.json`, `tools/wire-lock.ts`) holds those files, TDSP
1.x's frozen ones, the core's normative sections and the binding documents to their
recorded hashes, and asks a change of the draft's wire for a new marker (VER-3). TDSP 1.x's
vectors, `frames-v1.json` and `invitations-v1.json`, stay frozen beside the draft's; the 2.0
engine no longer reads them.

**Interoperability is checked against the published vectors, not shown between two
engines.** The published vectors are the wire's and the behaviour vectors that §3.6 names. One
independently written counterpart has run them: a small checker in Swift,
[conformance/swift-checker/](conformance/swift-checker/), by its authors' account written only from
the documents and the standards they name — parser, register merge, digests, clock — passes every vector but the
flows, which need an engine, and lists where the text left it to choose. It is no engine, so
the vectors show that this implementation and a second reading of the text agree on the wire,
not that two implementations interoperate. A statement about interoperability says "checked against the
published vectors", never "interoperable with a second engine".

## Known limits of the protocol

What TDSP does not promise, by design, for any implementation; the specification states each
where it applies: §10.6 for loss detection — closing a loss takes three steps, none promised by a
time (§15.4): it can be detected once a message shows it (an update that builds on what was lost,
or a report of someone who applied it), a resync is requested once that evidence lasts, and the
state is restored once an answer that holds it is applied; for control state a narrower limit
holds, since a member's edits and reports state no control clock —, and §15.4 for what a
participant whose software ignores the specification can do. This implementation was tested
against them as the rows above say.

- **Digests compare where the receiver holds the sender's state.** A lost deletion shows once the
  receiver holds every insertion the report counts, so after a lost insertion it shows only when
  that has arrived. A control digest is compared only at an equal control clock, and only in a
  report of a device of the creator (LOS-12), and a report of the creator whose clock lags is no
  evidence (LOS-10; `loss-detector.test.ts`). A report that crossed a deletion the receiver holds is
  a false alarm until its stream reports again, which it does at once on a request that states what
  it holds (SND-24), or answers the receiver whole (LOS-18; §10.6); meanwhile its gap rests after
  its tries and stays shown (LOS-6). Of a stream that no longer runs — its engine started anew —
  only the lapse ends it (LOS-12).
- **Control frames are no evidence, and a difference in digests lapses** (LOS-10, LOS-12, §10.6,
  §15.4). A `control` frame names no stream and no number that would order it among its device's
  frames, so control state is judged by reports alone: a lost decision shows only with the report
  that ends its device's phase, once that device pauses (10 s after its last edit or decision). Where the decision, its repeat and that report are all
  lost at a receiver, it stays there without evidence until a later report or a request makes the
  control state known, or an answer brings the missing registers; a restart creates that
  opportunity only where a report is owed or a start request falls due, and stored content sent
  again carries no rights. A difference in digests lapses once its stream was not heard for
  10 minutes, so a real one of a silent stream stays unseen until that stream reports again; one
  who writes on without a pause sends only `edit` frames, which name no stream, and the report that
  ends their phase brings such a difference back where it still holds. The 10 minutes are
  recommended, not required.
- **A register only members hold.** A decision of a lost device of the creator that reached
  members only, and none of her other devices, stays with them (CTL-46, §10.6, §15.4): such a
  member sees nothing while its control clock lies above theirs, and only once a later decision of
  the creator brings the clocks level does it see the digests differ and ask, once for each later
  decision of the creator: a request and an answer per decision, where only a content gap's cost
  is bounded to a request in 10 minutes (LOS-26); it passes the register on to every device it
  answers; only a new decision of the
  creator on that target, or that member's answer to a request of a device of the creator, heals
  it, and what a device of the creator merged so goes on only by its report, which it owes at once,
  a later request, a start request or its return (`creator-device.test.ts`).
- **Automatic requests switched off.** An engine whose application switched its automatic
  requests off (LOS-6, RSY-5) keeps its evidence until its person asks, or a start request or a
  return asks (RSY-1, RSY-21), which go all the same, as a joiner's first request does — a device
  still in its bootstrap, a member's or the creator's, asks again only as its bootstrap does, also
  when it returns —; its gaps stay until they heal (§10.6). That those requests go with them off,
  and that no gap asks and no bootstrap request is repeated, is tested (`start-request.test.ts`,
  `required-requests.test.ts`); the rest is argued.
- **A lost last change and its report.** A last change whose report was lost too, or that the
  report could not carry — split, or too large beside it (SND-27) —, shows by the report's state
  vector where that arrived (§10.6). Its sender opening the document again is an opportunity to
  heal it, no promise, in three cases only: the report was still owed (SND-28), and goes even where
  the device may no longer write; the device may still write and sends its stored content; or a
  start request falls due (RSY-1), on which a receiver that lacks the change asks all and the
  device answers, read-only or after the close too (RSY-10). A report the messenger accepted is
  owed no longer, however it fared, and after a revocation or the close no content goes; otherwise
  it shows once a later message of its sender builds on it, or once someone synchronises now
  (LIF-12). The three cases, and a report the transport accepted and then lost, are tested
  (`reopen.test.ts`).
- **A resting gap.** A gap rests after three tries and is woken only by a frame that carries its
  stream further or a frame of the member named as its writer, at most once in 10 minutes, or by
  a return or an absence longer than 10 minutes its application tells of, or the person (LOS-26); a change whose writer never returns
  and that nobody else holds stays missing, and shown. Gaps are not kept across a restart: after
  one, what the first read shows arises anew.
- **A bootstrap beyond what one answer carries** (TRN-23, §7.4, §15.4). Every transport carries
  at least 4 MiB, and an answer carries a control state at its bound beside the responder's state
  vector, which gains an entry for every session that wrote (LOS-27): roughly 75 000 sessions, an
  estimate whose assumptions §7.4 states — what decides is the bytes of the whole frame as
  encoded. Beyond it no answer carries the control state, nobody new is bootstrapped, and no state
  of the protocol says so; a device of the creator then decides nothing but a close without a
  reason (CTL-37) — read from the code, which measures that answer with the device's own state
  vector, and not tested at that size.
- **A missed close.** A member that lost the close, its repeat and the report after them while no
  device of the creator is online writes on until it asks or hears the close again, and everyone
  takes its edits (LIF-6, LIF-11, §15.4): a document closed as `continued` may still change.

- **Channel membership changes.** `channelMembers` (TRN-17) is a snapshot of who is in a
  channel when it is asked; no transport reports who joined or left, or when. An
  application learns of a change only by asking again and comparing, and misses someone
  who joined and left between two calls.
- **Channel versus control membership.** LIF-10 defines three combinations — a channel member
  no register names, a member whose register gives `read` or `write` who is not in the
  channel, and a revoked member who is still in it — as an inconsistency an application shows,
  a removal under way apart, and the engine compares them on request
  (`channelInconsistencies`). What it compares against is `channelMembers`, a snapshot (above).
  It compares registers on members (`member:<id>`) only: a member named only by a right on a
  layer counts as unnamed, though it may write that layer (LAY-5).
- **A restarted member goes on writing only through a cursor.** It edits again on its restored
  control state only where its transport offers `receiveSince` and its application persisted the
  cursor with the content (CTL-41, LIF-8); a transport that keeps its window only in memory answers a
  cursor from before its own restart as `truncated`, and the member then waits for an answer.
- **Persistence across restarts** of a creator's control state and of undistributed
  changes is required of an application (CTL-26, SND-21); a creator whose persisted state
  is lost is a new device: another device of the creator brings it up, and where none and no
  backup is left the document is closed and continued as a new one (CTL-27, CTL-46, RSY-14). An application that restores
  persisted content through `join()` need not keep the undistributed mark: the engine
  treats restored content as undistributed, at the cost of one message per restart.
- **A reset identity at a messenger.** A device's signature protects against a device a provider
  adds to an account, not against an identity the provider puts in place of the account's (§15.1).
  Against that holds only an identity a bridge remembered from the first contact and holds to, for
  room keys and frames alike, until a person agrees to another (BND-1, MTX-4); remembering is a
  SHOULD, and a bridge that does not remember protects nothing against a reset identity. This
  repository's Matrix bridge does not remember (below).

## Not yet specified

Parts of 2.0 whose text is not yet what an independent implementation needs, or that stay outside
the stable set of §14.

- **Resource budgets.** Hard, interoperable limits for decoding input size and work are
  given as recommended values (Appendix B), not normative bounds; nesting depth is
  bounded normatively (§1.6).
- **Binding wire profiles for interoperability.** The Matrix binding names its
  attachment format by reference — `EncryptedFile` v2 of the Matrix Client-Server API
  v1.19 —, with the fields and checks a bridge needs and example events whose hash and
  decryption were checked by hand, not by a test here. The email binding's exact
  MIME/OpenPGP layout is a draft: what the reference bridge writes, not specified for a
  second implementation and not part of 2.0's stable set (§14); its requirements are.
- **The local bridge interface** (§12.6, [bindings/local-bridge-interface.md](bindings/local-bridge-interface.md)) is optional and a draft, not part of 2.0's stable set (§14): body limits,
  paging, content types and caching headers are not normative; LBI-1 to LBI-6 stand as written.
- **Operations**: logging retention and redaction, and behaviour after a backup, restore
  or upgrade, have no minimum rule yet.

## Gaps of this implementation

What the specification asks and this repository does not do yet. The bindings' duties of this
repository's bridges belong here, not under the limits: each is a gap of these bridges, not of the
protocol.

- **The bridges keep their window only in memory.** After a bridge's restart every older cursor
  reads as `truncated` (TRN-18), so a member restarted with content waits for an answer.
- **The bridges and 2.0's bindings.** The bridges carry 2.0's frames unchanged and serve its
  transport profile, but implement of what the bindings changed or added only what the rows
  above say.
- **Matrix device verification.** Element is moving its clients to exclude devices their
  account has not signed (MSC4153), announced for October 2026, and MTX-4 requires the same of
  every bridge. The Matrix bridge sends only from a signed device and delivers only from signed
  devices, but **still shares room keys with every device**: matrix-sdk-crypto-nodejs 0.6.6
  shares only with signed devices (`CollectStrategy.IdentityBasedStrategy`) while it trusts the
  account's own identity, which it does only holding the account's private master key — tried
  against a local Synapse: refused with a device signed from outside, accepted where the machine
  made the identity itself — and the bridge keeps no such key. A device a homeserver added gets
  the room keys: it can read, not write. The bridge's own device is signed with the account's
  recovery key (`pnpm run matrix:confirm-device`,
  [docs/matrix-device-confirmation.md](docs/matrix-device-confirmation.md)); tested against
  a local Synapse, **not tried against Element itself**. Interactive (emoji) verification is
  not built: the crypto library the bridge uses offers none. Nor does the bridge remember a
  member's identity (MTX-4's SHOULD): a homeserver that resets a member's identity has its new
  devices signed, given the keys and their frames delivered like any other's. MTX-4 no longer
  allows frames of a Megolm session whose sending device could not be checked; whether the
  bridge's decryption still takes them is not checked here.
- **No adapter supplies a retention** (LIF-8). How long a transport keeps a message for a device
  that is away reaches the engine only from its application (`transportRetentionMs`); no adapter
  of this repository supplies it, and every binding states it as unknown, so on these transports
  a return (RSY-21) shows only through `rebound` or an application's own knowledge.
- **No bridge of this repository offers channel operations** (bindings §12.5, a MAY): adding or
  removing a person happens in the messenger's own client, which LIF-10 then shows as an
  inconsistency, so an application on these bridges alone cannot meet LIF-9.
- **The email bridge's memory of seen messages** grows with the thread (a few hundred
  bytes per mail); bounding it needs an IMAP UID position — the same position that would
  let the bridge's cursor (TRN-18), kept only in memory today, survive a restart.
- **A verified PGP mode** — a fingerprint the person confirms before joining — is not
  built.
- **Restored control state is checked for its form only.** What an application restores
  (`PersistedControlState`) counts as none when it is no state of registers; one of the right
  form is taken as given, and a wrong one changes only what this device refuses or answers,
  since it is the device's own (CTL-26).
- **Carried control state** (ARC-5, CTL-25): the engine offers no way yet to merge a snapshot
  an application carried from another device of the creator. An application can hand carried
  state over only as the device's own persisted state (`controlState`, CTL-26): that keeps
  whether it was bootstrapped there (`PersistedControlState.bootstrapped`, RSY-14); but the device continues under
  the tag the state names unless the application leaves `device` out, where ARC-5 asks for a tag
  of its own and a carried snapshot (a MUST). Restoring without a tag is tested (a new tag, with
  what it held: `control-restore.test.ts`); carrying is not. Without it, an application carries
  the content, and the device is a new device of the creator (CTL-27).

## Evidence not yet gathered

- **The 2.0 engine over this repository's bridges beyond a plain channel.** At L2 it runs over
  the Matrix bridges in an unencrypted room and over the email bridges without PGP; not in an
  encrypted room, not with PGP, and not over the Signal bridge, which has no local server and so
  only L4 (below).
- **A second implementation**: two separately implemented engines exchanging new documents,
  bootstrap, changes, deletions, rights and large answers, in both directions, are not shown,
  and TDSP 2.0's stable set does not wait for them; what stands in for them are the flow
  vectors of those case groups (§3.6), which only this engine has run so far (the Swift checker
  runs the others); nor is it shown that this core is simpler
  to implement than 1.x's. This version's engine is a rework of the reference engine, not an
  independent one.
- **L4 with the 2.0 engine.** No run against production Signal or a real email provider has been
  made with this repository's bridges and the 2.0 engine; the rows that cite L4 rest on TDSP 1.4
  and say so.
